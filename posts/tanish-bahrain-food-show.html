Food Show Review · Bahrain

The Flavors of the Khaleeji Region: Bahrain Through Tala Bashmi

By Tanish · afikra, featuring Chef Tala Bashmi

Title of the TV Show: afikra - MB (Series on food and drink from the Arab world)

Chef or Presenter's Name: Host: Mike; Guest: Chef Tala Bashmi

Country of Origin: Bahrain

Episode Title and Number: Bahraini Cuisine | Tala Bashmi

Length of the Episode: 50:33

Theme or Focus: The evolution of Bahraini cuisine from a "cuisine of necessity" to modern fine dining, and the quest to put Bahrain on the global culinary map.

Specific Culinary Traditions Showcased: Seafood preservation (salting, drying, rehydrating), communal eating (shredding fish to share), and sun-fermentation (Mahyawa).

Link and Resource Page: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gGay4zbWOJM

The episode explores the food culture of Bahrain and how the country’s geography, history, and cultural influences have shaped what people eat. One of the main ideas presented is that Bahrain’s identity is closely connected to its geography as a “tiny island.” Because Bahrain historically did not have an abundance of resources or wealth, its cuisine developed around necessity, sustenance, and making food last. The episode explains that Bahrain’s extreme heat and dry climate can make growing local produce difficult for much of the year, although tomatoes grow well during the winter. Because of its location and access to the sea, seafood became an important part of the Bahraini diet. People developed preservation methods such as salting, drying, and rehydrating fish and shrimp so food could last through long and demanding workdays.

The episode also demonstrates how Bahrain’s food has been shaped by trade and migration. Bahraini cuisine contains influences from Persian, Filipino, and South Asian cultures. One interesting example is Mahyawa, a fermented fish paste made from dried anchovies, spices such as coriander and cumin, and salt. The mixture is placed in a bottle and fermented in the hot sun for about a month. This preparation shows how traditional Bahraini cooking developed creative ways of preserving ingredients while also producing strong and distinctive flavors.

Two questions that came to mind while watching the episode were:

  • How might Bahraini cuisine have developed differently if the country had not depended so heavily on seafood and food preservation because of its geography?
  • I also wondered, as Bahrain becomes more modern and globally connected, how can traditional foods remain important without losing their original cultural meaning?

Traditional food is especially important to Bahrain’s identity because meals are connected to family and community. The episode describes Muhammar, a dish where one fish is shredded over rice so that an entire family can share it. This demonstrates how traditional meals were designed not only around flavor but also affordability and making limited resources serve many people. During Eid, more elaborate foods such as Guzi, baby lamb served over rice, become part of the celebration. These festive foods demonstrate how food can carry emotional and religious significance, especially during important family gatherings.

The episode also shows that Bahraini cuisine is evolving. Bashmi incorporates modern and playful foods, including a deconstructed peanut butter and jelly sandwich and her interpretation of the “Matilda cake.” These dishes demonstrate a current culinary trend of using advanced techniques to recreate childhood memories and feelings of joy. Overall, the episode presents Bahrain as a country where food represents resilience, cultural exchange, family, celebration, and modernization. Bahraini cuisine is therefore not simply a collection of traditional dishes; it is a reflection of the country’s geography and history while continuing to develop a confident identity in the modern culinary world.
