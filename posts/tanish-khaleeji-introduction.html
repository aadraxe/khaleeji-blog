Exploring the Khaleeji Region: Beyond the Burj Khalifa

By Tanish

Why are you interested in exploring this particular culture?

Honestly, the only thing I know about this region is the UAE: the Burj Khalifa, the futuristic skyline, and the way it's shown as a high-tech city built in the middle of a desert. That image made me curious. I don't know what the region was like before the skyscrapers, or who the people are beyond the headlines. I chose it precisely because I don't know anything about the region and would like to learn about it and the story behind this region rather than relying on one symbol.

What would you like to learn or discover about it?

I want to find out how a desert region became known for technology and innovation so quickly. What did life look like before that? What do people there value, and how do they hold on to their identity while the place changes so fast? I also want to research a bit more into the other countries which make up the Khaleeji region.

Which aspects of the culture are you especially curious about?

  • The history before the modern buildings and what daily life used to be like
  • How people lived in such a hot, dry environment
  • Whether traditional customs are still part of everyday life or mostly in the background
  • Language, clothing, and family life
  • How the region compares to the images I see online and in the media

What inspired you to learn about the people, history, and culture of your chosen country?

Seeing that I only have a surface level understanding of the region mainly through Dubai and popular media, I wanted to learn more about the region wholistically and look more into places like Bahrain, Oman, and Kuwait.

How would you like to celebrate, represent, or share this culture through gastronomy (food and culinary traditions)?

Ideally having a food demo in class would be the best way to go about sharing the culture as evidenced by the msemmen cooking demo. As such researching a traditionally significant food dish which has no dietary restrictions for people in the class would be used. Along with cooking in class I think it would be beneficial to share some stories behind the dish to help build a better connection.

What food, dish, ingredient, cooking practice, or food tradition might help you tell a story about this culture?

I chose luqaimat, small fried dough dumplings that are crisp on the outside and soft inside, usually drizzled with date syrup or sugar syrup. From what I've started reading, they're eaten across the Gulf, especially during Ramadan, Eid, and family gatherings, which makes them a good way to see what the countries share.

What questions do you hope your group will explore as you work together?

  • What is the Etymology of the term “Khaleeji” and what cultural significance does it have?
  • How do the countries that make up the region differ culturally?
  • What do dates, spices, and other ingredients say about the environment and trade history of the region?
  • How is the real region different from the way it's portrayed in the media?
